PLAYER: what the gods and their Called are really like. Commit without reading if you want the surprises.
No tables. Reading advances no time.

1 WHAT GODS ARE AND ARE NOT
- Real, limited, and paying for every act. Attention is their coin. A miracle anyone can see costs a god years of small favours elsewhere.
- They do not speak in sentences. They give certainty, dreams, coincidences that fall the right way, and, through the Called, power.
- They want things, and their wants conflict. None is good or evil as a whole. Each is narrow.
- They cannot simply see the fire. They notice it the way a landowner notices a new chimney: through reports from their people, and slowly.
- No god will rescue anyone on demand. Prayer mostly gets nothing visible.

2 THE CALLED
- About 30 alive across the three nations (1 in 200,000). Most are in cities, courts and great temples. A Called on the road is an event people talk about for a year.
- Power scale: a minor Called can do one thing reliably (close wounds, bless seed, make a witness unable to lie under oath). A great Called, of whom there are 4, can turn a battle or end a plague in a town.
- Limits: only in the god's province; only for the god's ends; it tires them like hard labour; a Called who acts against the god's nature loses it for a season or for good.
- They are people: proud, tired, frightened of their own gift, used by their churches.
- None appears before pressure tier 3 (gm/world_gm 3).

3 THE SEVEN
THARN, the Anvil-Father
- Wants: oaths kept, work done true. Hates: forgery, short weight, oath-breaking.
- Church: the Forge-Halls of Hald; each clan-hold keeps one. The High Hammer sits at Karrow Deep. Clerics are smiths first.
- Called (5 alive). Relevant: MOTHER ISGERD STONEHAND, dwarf, 141, Karrow Gate; binds oaths so that breaking them marks the breaker. Blunt, fair, bad knees.
- On the fire: the bowl beneath it is oath-work. Tharn's interest is that the oath be kept until it lawfully ends and then ended properly. His people would not steal it; Clan Brannoch, who are not his people so much as their own, might.

MAEDRA, the Sower
- Wants: fields tilled, heirs born, each thing in its place. Hates: waste land, bastardy of title, disorder.
- Church: the Furrow. Rich, literate, owns a fifth of Corrane. Archprelate at Corran itself. Runs parish records, tithes, grain loans. Acts for itself nine times in ten.
- Called (9 alive, the most). Relevant: BROTHER AUREN VEY, aasimar, 34, attached to the Aldermere chapter; blesses ground so it bears. Sincere, gentle, and unaware his superiors mean to plant him in the vale as a living deed of title.
- On the fire: an untended hearth on waste land is an affront and an opportunity. The Furrow will want it inside a parish.

ILVETH, the Tally-Keeper
- Wants: roads open, bargains honoured, risk priced. Hates: robbery, closed gates, bad coin.
- Church: guild chapels; no hierarchy, only chapters that audit each other. Shrine-wardens at crossroads are often retired carters.
- Called (6 alive). Relevant: PELL ODDFELLOW, halfling, 61, itinerant; knows whether a bargain struck in front of him will be kept. Cheerful, nosy, walks the three roads every year and is due through in June.
- On the fire: a crossroads with a light and no toll is close to holy. Ilveth's people will want a shrine post and will be useful, talkative friends.

NERYS, the Ferrywoman
- Wants: the dead named, buried and left in peace; crossings kept. Hates: unburied dead, grave-robbing, necromancy for gain.
- Church: the Grey Ferry; poor, respected, neutral. Burial-priests walk battlefields.
- Called (4 alive). Relevant: SISTER MAUVE, human, 50s, vale-born (Sarnford child, survived 719); quiets the restless dead. Comes each year for the Crossing (1 Nov) to the war pit. Says little. Will know at once that the fire is older than the ruin.
- On the fire: wary of anything that speaks with the dead (Speak, Sleeper). Respectful use, with burial and naming, earns her. Using the dead as informants without care makes an enemy of the one church everyone trusts.

KHELED, the Red Banner
- Wants: courage, loyalty under arms, a fair fight. Hates: cowardice, massacre of the helpless, desertion.
- Church: no temples; banner-shrines carried by companies and houses.
- Called (3 alive), all with armies. None relevant early.
- On the fire: indifferent until blood is shed at it. Then interested in who stood and who ran.

ORRACH, the Horned
- Wants: the hunt, the cull, winter taking the weak. Hates: fences, waste of a kill, pity that breeds weakness.
- Church: none among the nations; hunters' rites. Among goblins, gnolls and bugbears: shamans, a few of them truly Called (2 alive).
- Relevant: the north-rim goblins' shaman, OLD NIK-NIK, is not Called but thinks she is. In a starving winter she will read warmth as Orrach's bait or his gift, depending on who fed whom.
- On the fire: a thing that keeps the weak alive through winter. Not an enemy; a rival claim.

THE HEARTH-WIFE
- Wants: the fire kept, the guest fed, the door shut against the dark. Hates: guest-right broken, a cold hearth, a child turned away.
- Church: none. No Called has ever been known.
- Truth: she is the oldest of them and the weakest in the way gods count strength, because nobody builds her temples. Her power is that everyone, everywhere, keeps her one law a little.
- On the fire: it was her hearth for four hundred years before it was a treaty-bowl. What it is to her now is NOT DECIDED and must never be asserted in play. Signs of her are always deniable: a draught that shuts a door, bread that does not burn, a stranger who decides against it.

4 HOW CHURCHES ACTUALLY ARRIVE
- First a low-ranked person passing through who writes a letter.
- Then someone sent to look, with instructions to be polite.
- Then an offer: a shrine post, a blessing, a chaplain, a loan, a deed.
- Only then, if refused or outbid, pressure: sermons against the place, a boycott, a lawsuit, a rival hearth.
- Violence is last and deniable, and done by people who believe they are in the right.

5 MIRACLES NEAR THE FIRE
- A Called working inside act range is felt by the fire as heat of a different colour. No mechanics beyond that until needed.
- A Called can tell the fire is not a spirit, not a demon and not a god. What it is, they cannot tell.
- No Called can command it. Tharn's can read the oath in the bowl if they see the bowl.
