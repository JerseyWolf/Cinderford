10_WORLD CINDERFORD v4 - DRAFT (planning 23 Nov 2026; not canon until the player approves). PUBLIC: what anyone on the road could tell you, plus what Mercy himself knows (sections 3 and 4). No tables. Reading advances no time, grants nothing. Replaces 10_world v0 (the fire campaign) and drafts v1 and v2. Changed in v4: the War Meadow's dead (section 9). Changed in v3: road layout accepted; merchants' guards (section 6); crystals (section 9). Changed in v2: the roads (section 2) and the crossing lane (sections 2 and 3).
Lines marked [PROPOSED] were written by the narrator in planning and wait for the player's yes or no. Everything else is v0 text kept, or a player ruling of 23 Nov 2026.
Design rule (player): the world is realistic. People eat, pay, get tired and lie for ordinary reasons. Trade follows need. Distance costs days. Magic is rare enough that most people never see it.

1 THE SARN VALE
- Two river valleys join a third. The Upper Sarn comes down from the Hald uplands in the north-west. The Corrow comes up from the Corrane lowlands in the south-west. They meet and run east as the Lower Sarn to the League's lowlands.
- Valley floors: water meadow, alder and willow by the rivers, abandoned strip fields going to birch scrub. Slopes: oak, hornbeam and pine. North rim: steep, rocky, 300 to 400 m above the floor. South rim: lower, rolling, wooded.
- Soil is good. The fields are empty because of law and fear (section 3).
- People living in the whole vale today: a few hundred. Charcoal burners, shepherds, a ferryman's family downstream, squatters in half-roofed farmsteads, a few hermits. Before the war: about 4,000 in nine villages.
- Wildlife: deer, boar, wolves, beaver, lynx, an occasional bear from the north rim. Rarer and worse things in section 9.

2 THE CONFLUENCE AND THE TONGUE
- THE RIVERS. The Lower Sarn is about 150 m wide (think of the Vistula in Cracow). The Upper Sarn and the Corrow are smaller, 60 to 90 m each. None can be forded by a cart. All three carry flat-bottomed boats and barges: downstream under oar and current, upstream by pole, sail and tow-line.
- The Upper Sarn is fast and cold; it runs high and dangerous during snowmelt and after heavy rain (usually late March to mid April, and after July storms). The Corrow is slow, deep and muddy, with marshy banks. In a hard winter all three freeze and sledges cross on the ice.
- This is the only place where boats of all three nations can pass from one river to another. Every cargo that moves by water between Hald, Corrane and the League goes round the tip of the Tongue.
- THE TONGUE. A peninsula between the Upper Sarn (north side) and the Corrow (south side), pointing east to the meeting of the waters. About 600 m long and 250 m wide at the neck. Dry ground, a few metres above ordinary water. [PROPOSED: dimensions]
- THE NECK WALL. An old war wall crosses the neck from river to river, about 250 m. It is breached in three places. West of it the land rises toward the War Meadow (section 9).
- THE BRIDGES. Two old stone bridges still stand, both in poor repair: the North Bridge from the Tongue over the Upper Sarn, the South Bridge from the Tongue over the Corrow. They are the only bridges on any of the three rivers within about 40 km. Other bridges exist further out.
- THE ROADS (player ruling: the Tow Road has moved; every route needs a bridge). Layout (accepted by the player):
  - The Tow Road once ran along the south bank of the Lower Sarn. Marsh took that bank where the slow Corrow comes in. It now runs along the north bank and ends at the north end of the North Bridge.
  - The Iron Road cannot follow the east bank of the Upper Sarn: the crags of the north rim come down to the water there. It comes down the west bank, past the War Meadow, to the old gate in the neck wall.
  - The Grain Road comes up from the Corrane lowlands on a causeway to the south end of the South Bridge.
  - The three roads meet only on the Tongue. League to Hald: North Bridge and the neck gate. League to Corrane: both bridges. Hald to Corrane: the neck gate and the South Bridge.
- Nobody can pass the crossing without paying except by force. There are other roads and other bridges further out; this is the most direct route.
- THE CROSSING LANE. Travellers cross the Tongue on the old road between the neck gate and the two bridges, and nowhere else. [PROPOSED]
- WHAT STOOD HERE. The town of Sarnford filled the Tongue. It was sacked and burned in the war, in 719, and razed. Since then carters call the place Cinderford. [PROPOSED: the name Sarnford is older than the bridges and remembers a ford nobody alive has seen.]
- WHAT STANDS NOW. Barely anything. The earth has taken it back: house footings under nettles and birch; a choked well; the stump of a signpost; a fallen milestone; the roofless shell of the old toll-house; a burial ground with leaning markers; the two bridges; the breached wall. No roof on the whole Tongue.

3 THE PEACE AND THE SALE
- The War of the Ford (714 to 722) was fought by all three neighbours over this crossing. Nobody won. It ended with the Peace of the Three Seals in 722, sworn at the crossing itself.
- Under the Peace the vale is "held by none": no nation may keep a garrison, fortification, toll, court, tax collector or banner in it. Travellers of all three nations may pass.
- In practice: no law. Nobody hangs a robber here, and nobody is paid to stop one. Each nation's patrols stop at their own boundary stones. Carters call the vale the Leave, as in land that was left.
- Everyone old enough remembers the war. Most families within three days' travel lost someone here.
- THE SALE (player ruling). Mercy Goodheart, a healer in good standing with all three nations, asked each of them about this ground specifically and charmed them into selling it. Each nation's representative sold its claim to land it thought useless, meaning to cheat him, and sure the other two would never sanction it. All three signed. None of them should have: the land was meant to stay neutral.
- WHAT HE OWNS. The Tongue, and the facing banks of all three rivers. The deeds are genuine and carry all three seals. The rest of the vale is still held by none. [PROPOSED: the bank strips run 200 m deep and 1 km along each river from the confluence.]
- THE LAW AS IT NOW STANDS (player ruling). Mercy is a private owner holding a passage he legally owns, with bodyguards. The Peace binds the three nations; it says nothing about a private landowner. Raising the dead has never itself been a crime: every necromancer so far was a mass murderer, and it was the murder that was illegal. Corpses and spectres on ground that belongs to no nation are, in law, more or less fine. Priests dislike it.
- Politics will be slow. The treaty that all three broke by selling is the same treaty that stops each of them from marching in.
- THE PAPERWORK. Nobody lied. Each deed has a box for purpose of use. Mercy wrote: "Private toll and merchant enterprise." Workforce: "Local. All staff are long-term residents of the vale and are not expected to leave." Wages: "None anticipated." Dependants: "None surviving." Three clerks stamped it. Each nation believes it swindled a soft-hearted healer and that the other two were the fools. [PROPOSED: wording of the forms]
- Living people may settle, camp and trade on the banks. They are customers. Nobody but Mercy and his own enters the rest of the Tongue (player ruling); paying travellers keep to the crossing lane (section 2).

4 THE OWNER AND THE DEBT
- MERCY GOODHEART. Human, male, 35. 185 cm, 100 kg, brown hair, blue-silver eyes, rather good looking. A great healer whose healing is paid for with his own pain. He spent years mending adventurers who had been fools, for close to no recognition. He decided that healing and resurrection are basically necromancy, and changed careers. He wants a tower and for everyone to leave him alone, and he is a businessman who is not afraid to kill when someone interferes with his business.
- He once wrote a song as a joke, "I Can't Heal Dumb". It stopped being a joke.
- He is a living man and still has his humanity. Whether that holds is not known.
- Where he was born and which nation's subject he was: not set.
- THE LOANS (player ruling: one bank in each nation; none asked for details because of his good name; he told them it was a private merchant enterprise; he fully intends to pay). Figures [PROPOSED]:
  - Hald: the Karrow Assay House, a dwarven clan mint. 30,000 coins at 8 in the hundred: 2,400 a year. Sworn on Tharn's anvil.
  - Corrane: the Granary Chest of Aldermere, the Furrow's lending house. 24,000 coins at 10 in the hundred: 2,400 a year.
  - League: the Brass Scale Counting-House of Tollwick, gnome bankers. 36,000 coins at 12 in the hundred: 4,320 a year.
  - Total borrowed 90,000. Interest 9,120 a year, about 25 coins a day.
- TERMS. Two years' grace: nothing is owed and no interest runs until the first payment, due 25 March 755. After that, interest every year. The principal is never called in while the interest is paid (player ruling). What a lender may do after a missed payment: not set.
- CASH IN HAND on Day 1: 1,000 coins. The rest went on the land.

5 THE THREE NEIGHBOURS
Each needs something another has. That is why the crossing matters and why they do not trust each other.

5.1 THE KINGDOM OF HALD (north and north-west, up the Upper Sarn)
- Land: cold uplands and mountains. Thin soil, short summers, deep mines.
- Peoples: dwarves (most numerous; mine and forge clans), goliaths (high-pasture herders, porters, shock troops), gnomes (assayers, engineers, clockmakers), humans (valley farmers and carters, the poorest).
- Rule: a king elected for life by the clan-holds; real power sits with the mine clans and their debts to each other.
- Sells: iron, steel, salt, silver, copper, cut stone. Hald silver marks are the coin everyone trusts.
- Needs: grain. Hald cannot feed itself in a bad year, and 752 was a bad year.
- Temper: blunt, proud, slow to forgive, exact about contracts and weights.
- Nearest place: Karrow Gate, a walled border town, 2 days north by cart (about 45 km, the last 15 uphill).

5.2 THE CORRANE CROWN (south and south-west, up the Corrow)
- Land: wide lowlands, black soil, oak forest, big rivers. The oldest and most populous of the three.
- Peoples: elves (old landholding families; long memories, long lawsuits), humans (the great majority; tenant farmers and townsfolk), dragonborn (hereditary military houses), orcs (border clans granted land in return for mounted service), aasimar (few; mostly born into or taken by the church).
- Rule: a hereditary crown, a slow court, strong provincial lords, a church that owns a fifth of the land.
- Sells: grain, wool, flax, timber, horses, wine.
- Needs: iron and salt, which it has almost none of.
- Temper: courteous, hierarchical, patient, certain that the vale was always theirs.
- Nearest place: Aldermere, a market town with a grain exchange, 2 days south-west (about 50 km of flat, muddy road).

5.3 THE SARNMOUTH LEAGUE (east, down the Lower Sarn)
- Land: river delta, coast, canals. Seven chartered cities and the farmland that feeds them.
- Peoples: humans and halflings in about equal numbers (merchants, bargemen, craftsmen, market gardeners), gnomes (bankers, instrument makers, licensed mages), tieflings (old delta families; pilots, lawyers, dyers).
- Rule: a council of the seven cities; each city run by its guilds. No king. Wars are fought with hired companies.
- Sells: cloth, glass, paper, finished goods, shipping, credit and insurance.
- Needs: everyone else's goods to pass through its hands. The League charges toll on the lower Sarn at Tollwick. Cargo that passes between Hald and Corrane at the confluence pays the League nothing.
- Temper: quick, polite, litigious, counts everything.
- Nearest place: Tollwick, the League's upriver customs town, 3 days east by cart (about 70 km), or a day and a half downstream by boat.
