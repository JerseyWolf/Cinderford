== THE SARNMOUTH LEAGUE ==

NISSA FENWICKET - Senior Factor for upriver risk - THE BRASS SCALE COUNTING-HOUSE (League)
Faction: gnome bankers of Tollwick who lend, insure cargo on the lower Sarn and sell credit to all three nations. Holds Mercy's League loan: 36,000 at 12 in the hundred. Wants its 4,320 a year and to know everything a week before anyone else.
Gnome, 41, Tollwick. Under a metre; heart-shaped face; bright black eyes behind small gold spectacles; chestnut curls pinned up with pen nibs; an immaculate plum coat; dimples she deploys on purpose. Pretty, precise, and counting.
Fights: no. A pocket hand-crossbow, a hired bodyguard and a penalty clause.
Voice: quick, bright, delighted.
Speech: (League) "subject to", "per", figures to the penny; thinks aloud in percentages.
Swears: regular and light ("void it", "bugger the margin").
Samples: friendly "Oh, that is clever. That is so clever I shall have to charge you for it." / angry "You have changed my risk without telling me. Twelve was a courtesy." / everyday "Per the schedule, four thousand three hundred and twenty. I brought my own scales." / fright "Void it, void it. Which way is out, and what does it cost?" / stressed: recalculates aloud, faster each time.
Life: prices risk for a living. The Brass Scale also insures the barges that his toll will now stop, so every boat he counts moves two columns in her book. The clerk who lent on his good name has been promoted out of reach.
Off duty: builds tiny orreries; plays cards for matchsticks and wins; collects clocks that disagree.
Ties: the rate war with Dagna Orrik; feeds numbers to Calista Varr and buys law from her; owes Briar Tolliver for road news.
Toward Mercy at start: the most interesting entry in her book. Wants to know whether to call the risk up or sell him more.
Flaws: cannot resist a clever instrument; treats a disaster as data. Weak side: no physical courage at all. Annoys others: corrects their sums while they are still speaking.
Disposition: friendly as policy, charmed by novelty; real fondness, when it comes, she books as an unexpected overhead.
Romance: flirts as a negotiating habit and is flustered when it is taken at face value. Wants terms discussed first, and enjoys the discussing.

CALISTA VARR - River Proctor - THE TOLLWICK CUSTOMS BOARD (League)
Faction: the League's upriver customs, answerable to the council of the seven cities. It charges the League's toll on the lower Sarn. Wants a ruling on whether a private toll on water "held by none" is lawful, a count of every boat, and no precedent that costs the League a penny.
Tiefling, 36, an old delta family of pilots, lawyers and dyers. Dusk-red skin; swept-back horns ringed with thin silver bands; black hair in one long plait; solid amber eyes; a tail she has trained to keep still; tailored black and river-blue. Arresting, and aware of the effect on a tribunal.
Fights: rapier, when procedure fails. Holds a pilot's licence and handles her own boat.
Voice: low, amused, cross-examining.
Speech: (League, legal) "for the record"; questions she already knows the answer to; "my learned friend" for anyone she is about to dismantle.
Swears: rare and elegant ("tally take it").
Samples: friendly "For the record, that was well argued. I shall deny having said so." / angry "You will find, Master Goodheart, that I have read your deed more carefully than you have." / everyday "How many hulls since the first of the month? Take your time. I have the figure." / fright "Tally take it. Cut the line. Cut it." / stressed: the tail moves.
Life: took silk young and has not lost a river case. Sent upriver to settle a question the council would rather stayed unsettled for years. Dyes her own silks, a family habit.
Off duty: sails a small boat alone; fences; reads other nations' law for pleasure.
Ties: buys figures from Nissa Fenwicket; the polite war with Elowen Vause; Briar Tolliver's fellowship has sued the Board twice and Calista won both; has read Sylwen Aldaine's thirty-year lawsuit for pleasure.
Toward Mercy at start: a legal curiosity with a tower. Wants to find the clause he did not read.
Flaws: has to win; hides behind procedure; a delta snob. Weak side: cannot leave a question unanswered, so she can be baited. Annoys others: takes notes while they talk.
Disposition: cool and ironic. Her respect is won by out-arguing her once, fairly.
Romance: drawn to whoever beats her in argument, and irritated by it. Discreet; never while a case between them is open.

== BELONGING TO NONE ==

HOLLIS WRENNE - Burial-priest of the vale - THE FERRY ORDER (none)
Faction: Nerys's burial priests, who bury the dead of any nation and are let through every line. They keep the book of the vale's dead. Wants the dead named, buried and left alone.
Human, 38, vale-born. Tall and lean; weathered fair skin; river-grey eyes; dark blonde hair cropped under a grey hood; strong hands; a cord of pierced coins at her belt. Austere good looks that she has no use for.
Fights: no. A ferry-pole staff. Whether she is Called is not public.
Voice: quiet, plain, unafraid.
Speech: (vale) few words; "the Leave"; she uses the names of the dead as if they were in the room.
Swears: regular and dry ("drown it"; shit, flatly).
Samples: friendly "You put a coin under his tongue. I saw. I'll write that down too." / angry "He had a name. Use it, or I will, loudly, at your gate." / everyday "Three in the meadow since the thaw. Two I know. One I'll have to ask about." / fright "Drown it. Back to the water. It won't cross." / stressed: counts the coins on her cord.
Life: her family's village was one of the nine; she grew up among the markers. Walks the vale with a spade and the book. The Sarnford burial ground and the War Meadow are hers in conscience, whatever the deeds say.
Off duty: fishes; mends nets; drinks spirits neat and says little.
Ties: let through by every patrol, so she knows Oksa High-Cairn and Mazga Tal-Korra both; honoured and ignored by Elowen Vause; Briar Tolliver's carters send for her; Sylwen Aldaine gives her the names she is missing.
Toward Mercy at start: the first person to read his deed forms properly. Has not decided whether he is a grave-robber or a new kind of sexton.
Flaws: stubborn, judging, tired. Weak side: she has no power but standing, and knows it. Annoys others: says the names.
Disposition: hard to move and fair. Cannot be bought. Can be reasoned with, slowly, by someone who answers her questions straight.
Romance: not looking, and plain about it. If it ever came it would be late, quiet and after trust; a man who raises her dead starts a long way back.

KAZRA VOSK - Captain - THE NINTH ARTICLE COMPANY (none)
Faction: a hobgoblin hired company that fights for whoever pays and keeps its contracts to the letter. Hired most autumns to guard convoys. Wants pay, reputation and winter quarters.
Hobgoblin, 34, born in the company's baggage train. Brick-red skin; high cheekbones; yellow eyes; black hair in a soldier's knot; lacquered armour without a mark on it; a duelling scar through one eyebrow. Severe and striking.
Fights: glaive in the shield line; commands better than she fights, and she fights well.
Voice: clipped, exact, parade-level.
Speech: (company) "per article"; ranks instead of names; "the Company" where others say "I".
Swears: regular, in a drill voice ("shit on parade").
Samples: friendly "The Company notes that you paid before the day. That is entered." / angry "Article four. You were read it. The Company does not explain twice." / everyday "Sixty on the road, forty in camp, watch changes at the bell." / fright "Shit on parade. Close up. Close up!" / stressed: inspects kit that passed inspection an hour ago.
Life: earned the captaincy by vote of the sergeants. Any of the three nations might hire her against him, and so might he. She would serve either contract the same way.
Off duty: copies the company's articles in a fine brush hand; plays a stones-and-board game against her lieutenant; bathes more than anyone thinks reasonable.
Ties: wary mutual respect with Oksa High-Cairn; Mazga Tal-Korra wants to hire her; Briar Tolliver's convoy masters are her usual paymasters and haggle her fee every year; will not take Rhezka Ashmantle's coin, on principle.
Toward Mercy at start: a possible employer, a possible target, and a possible landlord for the winter. No opinion on the dead beyond their drill.
Flaws: rigid about the letter of a contract; contempt for amateurs; never forgets a slight to the Company. Weak side: off contract she does not know what to do with herself. Annoys others: inspects their kit and tells them the result.
Disposition: formal, then loyal inside the terms. Her humour is bone-dry and easy to miss.
Romance: never with an employer while a contract runs: it is in the articles. Off contract she is direct, a little formal about it, and surprised to be asked.

BRIAR TOLLIVER - Convoy-Mistress - THE AXLE AND OAR (none)
Faction: the fellowship of carters, drovers and bargemen of every people who work the three roads and rivers. It has no flag. It decides whether the convoys pay at the crossing or go the long way round. Wants a fair rate, a safe camp and nothing dead near the oxen.
Halfling, 30, born on a League barge, raised on the road. About a metre tall; sun-browned and freckled; honey-brown curls under a carter's cap; green eyes; a quick grin; strong forearms; a waistcoat brighter than the weather. Very pretty and haggles with it.
Fights: sling and a drover's whip; mostly she has forty carters and whoever they hired.
Voice: fast, warm, bargaining before she has said hello.
Speech: (road) "what's the damage"; axle and tow-line words; a nickname for everyone inside a minute.
Swears: heavy and cheerful (fuck, bollocks, "axle-grease").
Samples: friendly "Right, you. Five a cart's robbery, three's friendship. Which are we?" / angry "You held my wagons a day. That's forty oxen eating. I'll remember the fucking number." / everyday "Roads' Opening's the twenty-third. I've sixty carts want to know your price before they grease an axle." / fright "Bollocks. Get the beasts round. Round! Don't let them see it." / stressed: talks faster and starts feeding people.
Life: speaks for the convoy masters because she can count, shout and be liked in the same breath. Knows every inn, landing and bad bridge for a week in any direction.
Off duty: dice; singing; cooks for whoever is in camp; collects gossip like other people collect debts.
Ties: beats Oksa High-Cairn at dice; sells road news to Nissa Fenwicket; has lost twice in court to Calista Varr and has not forgiven it; pays Kazra Vosk every autumn and argues the fee; half her carters have carried something for Rhezka Ashmantle and she pretends not to know.
Toward Mercy at start: a new toll, which is to say a new cost. Wants a season rate and to see what he is like when someone says no.
Flaws: a gossip; cannot keep a secret that makes a good story; holds a grudge over cheated weights. Weak side: soft for a hard-luck story. Annoys others: the nicknames.
Disposition: friendly at once, loyal slowly. Fair dealing wins her; being cheated once loses her for a year.
Romance: easy and playful, flirts with everyone and means about a tenth of it. Anything real would be common knowledge on three roads within a week, and she knows it.

SYLWEN ALDAINE - Speaker of the Moot - THE NINE VILLAGES (none)
Faction: the vale folk: a few hundred squatters, shepherds, charcoal burners and ferry families, descendants of the nine villages, with no lord and no tax. They meet as a moot when something must be decided. They have lived on this land without leave for thirty years, and some of it is now his. Want to be left where they are, and to know what happens to their dead.
Elf, 139 (looks about thirty), born in Sarnford. Tall and slender; pale skin; silver-blonde hair worn in a long loose braid; dark grey eyes; a patched green gown that was fine cloth fifty years ago, kept clean; an old signet she does not show. Beautiful in the way of a house that is still standing.
Fights: a longbow she has hunted with for a century. Will not use it on people if there is any other way.
Voice: calm, low, exact about dates.
Speech: (old Corrane, worn down by the vale) full sentences, old courtesies, "in my father's time"; she corrects place-names.
Swears: almost never. "Blight" for a late frost. When she is truly angry she goes very polite.
