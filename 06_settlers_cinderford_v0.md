1 HOW SETTLERS WORK
- Three states: PASSING (a night or two), STAYING (days to weeks, no promise), SETTLED (calls the place home; semi-permanent).
- Nobody settles because the fire wants it. People stay for their own reasons and leave for their own reasons. The narrator never makes them stay to be convenient.
- Room cap: SETTLED people need somewhere to sleep. Each sleeping place in 07_buildings holds a stated number. No roof, no settlers beyond what a camp can bear.
- Deep cast: about 5 settlers at a time are written in full (history, flaw, want, fear, habits, secrets in the GM file). Others are background until one earns a place.
- Away: settlers leave on their own business (work, trade, trouble, adventures) and come back with coin, goods, wounds, news or company. What happened is resolved out of sight and told in their own words, which may not be the whole truth.
- Death is permanent. So is leaving for good.
- What they know about the fire: at first, only that it never goes out and that people sleep well beside it. Sharp or curious people may notice more and test it. Each forms their own belief; beliefs can be wrong.
- Standing toward the fire and toward each other: wary -> cool -> neutral -> friendly -> warm -> trusted. Changes only through play.
- No romance with the fire. What settlers do among themselves is their business and stays in the background.
- Each settler entry here grows only by what the fire has seen or heard. New facts are added at saves.

2 ENTRY FORMAT
NAME (as the fire knows it) · people · rough age · trade · state
- Seen: appearance and manner.
- Says: what they have said about themselves at the fire.
- Does: habits the fire has watched.
- Wants (as far as anyone can tell).
- Standing: toward the fire; toward others.
- Away log: where they said they went; what they came back with.

3 THE FOUNDER (public sketch for player veto; nothing below is known to the fire until it happens in play)
NAME: not yet heard. · half-orc (orc father, human mother) · mid forties · cook · not yet arrived
- Seen: broad and short for her father's people, tall for her mother's. Grey-green skin, weathered. One lower tusk chipped. Black hair going iron-grey, tied up in a cloth. Burn scars on both forearms, the ordinary kind cooks have. Walks like someone who has pushed a loaded handcart a long way and resents every stone. Voice carries.
- Kit: a two-wheeled handcart; an iron cook-pot big enough for twenty; a griddle; knives in a leather roll that she never lets out of reach; sacks; a tarred canvas; not much else.
- Manner: blunt. Swears fluently in three languages. Quick to anger, quicker to feed whoever she just shouted at, and will not apologise in words. Cannot watch someone do a kitchen job badly. Does not take charity and does not offer it: she trades.
- Background she will tell anyone: nineteen years cooking for a carrying company that ran all three roads. Fed Hald dwarves, Corrane riders and League clerks out of the same pot and watched them refuse to sit together. Left the company last autumn.
- Why here: she kept dreaming of a fire at a crossroads. She is annoyed that it turned out to be real, and more annoyed that she walked here to check.
- Wants: one table where all three nations eat together and nobody draws a knife. She would not put it that grandly. She would say she is sick of cooking three suppers.
- Flaws the fire will see quickly: temper; pride; a sharp tongue aimed at people who could help her; works herself past sense; holds a grudge.
- Toward the fire: treats it like a good stove. Talks to it while she works, the way cooks talk to ovens. Leaves it the first crust by habit.
- First aim on arrival: a roof over the cook-pot.
